# Chicago notes-bibliography: research for `chicago-notes.md` (betterpaper Phase 1)

Researched 2026-10-06. Italics are marked with `*...*`. All page numbers, URLs-in-examples and dates in examples are illustrative.

## 0. Method, edition, and confidence (read first)

- **Edition used: CMOS 18th (2024), the current edition.** 17th-vs-18th differences are flagged inline and summarised in section 12.
- **Access limit:** direct page fetches were blocked by the network egress proxy for every domain tried (chicagomanualofstyle.org, owl.purdue.edu, and about 10 library-guide sites). I could NOT read the official CMOS Quick Guide page itself. Every claim below comes from search-engine summaries of the cited URLs plus my own CMOS knowledge.
- **Tags:** `[S]` = stated in a search-result summary of the cited URL. `[U]` = UNCONFIRMED (not found in any public source this session; from memory or inferred). Treat `[U]` items as "verify before the reviewer hard-fails a student on it".
- **Reviewer stance:** accept both 17th and 18th forms if the essay is internally consistent. Do not flag a missing place of publication (optional in 18th).
- Official pages (summary only, unread): Quick Guide https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-1.html ; What's New in 18 https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/help-tools/what-s-new.html

## 1. Book, single author

- Note: `First Last, *Title: Subtitle* (Publisher, Year), page.`
- Short: `Last, *Short Title*, page.` (title shortened to 4 words or fewer, keeping the key noun)
- Bib: `Last, First. *Title: Subtitle*. Publisher, Year.`
- Example note: Charles Darwin, *On the Origin of Species* (John Murray, 1859), 112.
- Example short: Darwin, *Origin of Species*, 114.
- Example bib: Darwin, Charles. *On the Origin of Species*. John Murray, 1859.
- 18th vs 17th: 17th wrote `(London: John Murray, 1859)` and `London: John Murray, 1859.` 18th does not require the place [S]. Purdue's 18th page still shows a place in one example (Kerouac, `New York: Viking Press, 1958`), so both appear in the wild.
- Source: https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/books.html [S, template and Kerouac example]

## 2. Translated book

- Note: `First Last, *Title*, trans. First Last (Publisher, Year), page.`
- Short: `Last, *Short Title*, page.`
- Bib: `Last, First. *Title*. Translated by First Last. Publisher, Year.`
- Example note: Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*, trans. Gregory Hays (Modern Library, 2002), 45.
- Example short: Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*, 46.
- Example bib: Marcus Aurelius. *Meditations*. Translated by Gregory Hays. Modern Library, 2002.
- Rules: `trans.` abbreviated in notes, "Translated by" spelled out in the bibliography, translator's name not inverted [S].
- **Cite the translation actually read:** the entry gives the translator and the publisher/year of the edition in hand, and page numbers refer to that edition. Every guide's template is built this way [S as template]; an explicit CMOS sentence stating it was not found [U]. Reviewer check: translated work with no translator named, or pages that cannot belong to the cited edition.
- Original year: Chicago has a pattern for reprints, `(Publisher, 1952; repr., Publisher, 1995)` [S], and an older bib pattern `1911. Reprint, New York: Dover, 1966.` [S, via Zotero forum]. A slot for the original-language year of a translation inside the note is [U]. Safe advice: put it in running text ("Hegel's 1807 *Phenomenology*") or omit.
- Sources: https://lib.trinity.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/18th-Edition_Chicago-Notes-Bibliography-Style.pdf ; https://ustpaul.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/chicago-18-english-january-2026-1.pdf ; https://guides.douglascollege.ca/Chicago18/books ; https://libguides.st-andrews.ac.uk/Referencing_styles/chicago/notesandbibliography/18th/translated (all appeared in results; summaries gave `trans.` / "Translated by" and a Liu Xinwu example)

## 3. Chapter in an edited volume

- Note: `First Last, "Chapter Title," in *Book Title*, ed. First Last (Publisher, Year), page.`
- Short: `Last, "Short Chapter Title," page.`
- Bib: `Last, First. "Chapter Title." In *Book Title*, edited by First Last, first–last page. Publisher, Year.`
- Example note: Kathleen Doyle, "The Queen Mary Psalter," in *The Book by Design*, ed. P. J. M. Marks and Stephen Parkin (University of Chicago Press, 2023), 64. [S]
- Example short: Doyle, "Queen Mary Psalter," 65. [S]
- Example bib (range illustrative): Doyle, Kathleen. "The Queen Mary Psalter." In *The Book by Design*, edited by P. J. M. Marks and Stephen Parkin, 64–75. University of Chicago Press, 2023.
- 18th vs 17th: the chapter page range in the bibliography is now optional [S]; 17th required it. In the note, `ed.` is abbreviated; in the bib, "edited by" is spelled out [U, standard Chicago pattern, consistent with the examples].
- Sources: https://www.apsu.edu/writingcenter/writing-resources/chicago-18th-notes-bibliography-handout.pdf ; https://ustpaul.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/chicago-18-english-january-2026-1.pdf

## 4. Journal article (print and DOI)

- Note: `First Last, "Article Title," *Journal* vol, no. issue (Year): page, https://doi.org/xxxx.`
- Short: `Last, "Short Title," page.`
- Bib: `Last, First. "Article Title." *Journal* vol, no. issue (Year): first–last page. https://doi.org/xxxx.`
- Print only: same, with no DOI or URL. Cite the page you used in the note (not the full range); the bib gives the full range.
- Example note (2 authors): Lynda Mulvin and Steven E. Sidebotham, "Roman Game Boards from Abu Sha'ar (Red Sea Coast, Egypt)," *Antiquity* 78, no. 301 (2004): 603, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003598X00113250.
- Example short: Mulvin and Sidebotham, "Roman Game Boards," 605.
- Example bib: Mulvin, Lynda, and Steven E. Sidebotham. "Roman Game Boards from Abu Sha'ar (Red Sea Coast, Egypt)." *Antiquity* 78, no. 301 (2004): 602–17. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003598X00113250. [S for the bib entry with range 602-617; I applied the 9.61 elision]
- DOI rule: write it as the full `https://doi.org/...` link, after the page numbers [S]. A database name or stable URL replaces it when no DOI exists [S, UCD guide].
- 17th vs 18th: structure unchanged [S]. Authors: 18th lists at most 2 in a note (more than 2 means first + "et al."), at most 6 in the bibliography (more than 6 means first 3 + "et al.") [S]. 17th allowed more names (about 3 in a note, up to 10 in the bib) [U].
- Sources: https://libguides.ucd.ie/chicagostyle/journals ; https://guides.douglascollege.ca/chicago/articles ; https://lonestar-online.libguides.com/chicago17/examples ; https://lonestar-online.libguides.com/chicago/examples

## 5. Online source: web page, blog, Substack-style post

- Web page note: `Author or Org, "Page Title," Site Name, Month Day, Year [or "accessed Month Day, Year" if undated], URL.`
- Web page short: `Author or Org, "Short Page Title."`
- Web page bib: `Last, First (or Org). "Page Title." Site Name. Month Day, Year. URL.` (CMOS often lets web pages live in notes only [U on exact wording]; betterpaper should not flag a missing bib entry for a web page alone.)
- Date rule: use the publication or last-revised date; add an access date only if no such date can be found [S, UQ guide]. Expanded guidance on shortening long URLs in 18th [S].
- Example note (undated, placeholder URL): Maritime Heritage Trust, "Lighthouse Keepers' Logs," Maritime Heritage Trust, accessed March 3, 2026, https://example.org/logs.
- Blog note [S pattern]: `Author, "Post Title," *Blog Name* (blog), Month Day, Year, URL.` Example from guide: Elisabetta Boaretto, "Uncovering varied pathways to agriculture," Ancientfoods (blog), 20 December 2017, https://ancientfoods.wordpress.com/2017/12/20/uncovering-varied-pathways-to-agriculture/ (the guide's date order is day-first; CMOS 18 itself uses Month Day, Year in US usage).
- **Substack-style post** [S, summary attributed to CMOS Q&A]: treated like a blog post, newsletter name in italics, then the platform name. Pattern: `Author, "Post Title," *Newsletter Name*, Substack, Month Day, Year, URL.`
- Example (invented): Ada Quill, "On Keeping Bees," *The Slow Hive*, Substack, May 2, 2025, https://example.substack.com/p/on-keeping-bees.
- Substack short and bib forms: `Quill, "On Keeping Bees."` (note); bib `Quill, Ada. "On Keeping Bees." *The Slow Hive*, Substack, May 2, 2025. URL.` Both are by analogy with the blog pattern, [U] as separately published forms.
- Sources: https://guides.library.uq.edu.au/referencing/chicago-18th-edition-notes-and-bibliography/webpage ; https://guides.library.uq.edu.au/referencing/chicago-18th-edition-notes-and-bibliography/blog ; https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/Documentation/faq0473.html (Substack, summary only)

## 6. "Quoted in" (secondary citation)

- Policy: discouraged; cite the original whenever you can. Use only when the original is unavailable. The note names the original source first, then the secondary source where the quote was found [S].
- Note: `[Full note of original source, page], quoted in [full note of secondary source, page].`
- Example note: Louis Zukofsky, "Sincerity and Objectification," *Poetry* 37 (February 1931): 269, quoted in Bonnie Costello, *Marianne Moore: Imaginary Possessions* (Harvard University Press, 1981), 78. (Guide's 17th-style version had `Cambridge, MA: Harvard...`; place dropped per 18th.)
- Short: `Zukofsky, "Sincerity," 269, quoted in Costello, *Marianne Moore*, 78.` [U, derived by applying the short-form rule to both halves]
- Bibliography: list the secondary source you actually read (Costello). Whether the original must also be listed is disputed: one summary said both [S], common library practice says the consulted source only. UNCONFIRMED in CMOS text [U]. Reviewer check: a "quoted in" note whose secondary source is missing from the bibliography.
- Sources: https://guides.douglascollege.ca/chicago/secondary ; https://libanswers.snhu.edu/faq/415039 ; https://libguides.tru.ca/chicago/indirectsources

## 7. Ibid. policy

- 17th: ibid. discouraged in favour of shortened notes (CMOS 14.34), because electronic formats may show notes out of order; still permitted [S]. https://guides.douglascollege.ca/chicago/shortened-citations
- 18th: default subsequent citation is the author-title short note; two further short forms are named: author-only (when one work by that author is cited) and title-only. "Ibid." is still permitted but discouraged, so keep it optional [S; one summary says "discourages", another says "still permits"; these agree]. https://libguides.up.edu/chicago/short_form ; https://libguides.snhu.edu/c.php?g=307818&p=5880541 ; https://library.ulethbridge.ca/chicagostyle/guidelines/notes
- If Ibid. is used: only when the immediately preceding note cites exactly one source; add the page if it differs (`Ibid., 52.`); capital I with a period [S for "exact same source" condition, rest U]. Reviewer: flag non-adjacent ibid., ibid. after a note with two sources, lowercase "ibid", and "op. cit." / "loc. cit." (not Chicago [U]).
- Betterpaper default: do NOT mark ibid. as an error by itself; recommend shortened notes.

## 8. Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason* (A/B and Akademie)

- CMOS has no Kant-specific rule. Its classic-works guidance covers ancient, medieval, and sacred texts cited by standard divisions (book, chapter, line) instead of pages, normally in notes only [S, general content]. https://uvflibraryguides.valleyforge.edu/chicago-turabian-format-style-guide/older-works-and-sacred-works/ . Philosophy convention extends the same idea to Kant.
- Convention: the first *Critique* is cited by the pagination of the 1781 (A) and 1787 (B) editions, not by the translation's page numbers; passages in both editions get both, `A51/B75`; A-only `A51`; B-only `B75` [S]. Sources: https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/kant-immanuel-1724-1804/v-1/bibliography/kant-immanuel-1724-1804-bib ; https://philpapers.org/sep/kant-reason/
- All other Kant works are cited by Akademie volume and page, `4:421` (Groundwork, vol. 4) and `5:` (second *Critique*, vol. 5); the Ak. numbers sit in the margins of modern translations [S]. Some writers prefix "Ak." (`Ak. 4:421`), declared once [U].
- First note: Immanuel Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, trans. Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood (Cambridge University Press, 1998), A51/B75. (Cambridge edition, 1998 [S].)
- Short: Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, A51/B75.
- Bib: Kant, Immanuel. *Critique of Pure Reason*. Translated by Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood. Cambridge University Press, 1998. (Check the title page: if it says "translated and edited by", use "Edited and translated by" [U].)
- Groundwork first note, same pattern: `Immanuel Kant, *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*, trans. [translator] (Publisher, Year), 4:421.`
- Reviewer checks: "p. A51" or "pp." before A/B numbers; using the translation's page for the CPR with no A/B; A/B citing a work other than the first *Critique*; mixing `A51/B75` and `A51-B75`; repeating the full note when a short note would do.

## 9. Deleuze and Guattari: ATP, AO, WP

- Abbreviations in the literature: **AO** = *Anti-Oedipus*, **ATP** (also written **TP**) = *A Thousand Plateaus*, **WP** = *What Is Philosophy?* [S: Culp, *Dark Deleuze* lists AO, TP, WP; a result summary also saw ATP]. Which of ATP or TP is more common was not established [U]. Accept either if defined and consistent. https://manifold.umn.edu/read/dark-deleuze/section/7e4b4e26-3438-485f-bb27-7a22a7e60613 ; https://www.protevi.com/DG/Fall2013Intro.pdf (appeared in results, abbreviations not confirmed from it)
- CMOS mechanism: give the full citation in the first note, then say the abbreviation will be used, e.g. `(hereafter cited as ATP)`; later notes use the abbreviation plus page [S via https://proofed.com/writing-tips/frequently-cited-sources-in-chicago-footnote-referencing/amp , a commercial editing site; the official CMOS section number was not confirmed, [U]]. With many abbreviations, a short "Abbreviations" list before the notes is common (summary said 10 or more sources, [U] as a CMOS threshold).
- Edition data (library records [S]): ATP trans. Brian Massumi, University of Minnesota Press, 1987; AO trans. Robert Hurley, Mark Seem, and Helen R. Lane, University of Minnesota Press, 1983 (French 1972); WP trans. Hugh Tomlinson and Graham Burchell, Columbia University Press, 1994. Other editions exist (e.g., AO Viking 1977), so abbreviated page numbers are valid only for the edition cited. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Oedipus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Thousand_Plateaus
- First note ATP: Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, *A Thousand Plateaus: Capitalism and Schizophrenia*, trans. Brian Massumi (University of Minnesota Press, 1987), 12 (hereafter cited as *ATP*). Later: *ATP*, 25. (or Deleuze and Guattari, *ATP*, 25.)
- First note AO: Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, *Anti-Oedipus: Capitalism and Schizophrenia*, trans. Robert Hurley, Mark Seem, and Helen R. Lane (University of Minnesota Press, 1983), 5 (hereafter cited as *AO*).
- First note WP: Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, *What Is Philosophy?*, trans. Hugh Tomlinson and Graham Burchell (Columbia University Press, 1994), 35 (hereafter cited as *WP*).
- Bib (ATP): Deleuze, Gilles, and Félix Guattari. *A Thousand Plateaus: Capitalism and Schizophrenia*. Translated by Brian Massumi. University of Minnesota Press, 1987. Two authors are both named in a note (limit is 2 in 18th [S]); in the bib, the second name is not inverted. Repeat the authors' names for the next D&G entry, not a 3-em dash (18th) [S].
- Whether the abbreviation itself is italic is [U] (scholarship usually italicises it as a title abbreviation; be lenient). Parenthetical in-text `(ATP 25)` after definition is used in some humanities fields; CMOS 18 wording not confirmed [U]. Do not flag it if defined, but notes are the Chicago-NB norm.
- Reviewer checks: abbreviation used before being defined; abbreviation not in the first full note; inconsistent TP/ATP; missing translator; "What is Philosophy?" lower-case "is" (headline style capitalises it).

## 10. Page ranges

- Separate with an en dash (a hyphen is tolerated) [S]. CMOS 9.61 (17th) elision [S]:
  - Up to 99, and multiples of 100: all digits (3–10, 71–72, 96–117, 100–104, 1100–1113).
  - 101–109, 201–209, etc.: changed part only (101–8, 808–33, 1103–4).
  - Otherwise: two digits unless more are needed (321–28, 498–532, 1087–89, 1496–500, 11564–615, 12991–3001).
  - 18th presumably unchanged [U]. Source: https://cmosshoptalk.com/2018/04/10/316-7-316-17-or-316-317-chicago-style-for-number-ranges/
- No "p." / "pp." in notes (bare number after the final comma) [U, universal in the NB examples above]. Chicago prefers exact ranges over "f." / "ff." [U].
- Cite the page of the passage in the note; the bibliography gives the whole range for articles and (optional in 18th) chapters.

## 11. Block quotes and note-number placement

- Length: in 17th, CMOS 13.10 says "a hundred words or more can generally be set off" and shorter quotes (especially fragments) run in; multi-paragraph, correspondence, lists, and poetry also qualify [S]. "Five lines or more" is a Turabian-style and library-guide heuristic, not CMOS's own wording [S: guides say "five lines or longer, or exceeds 100 words"]. betterpaper rule: flag only clear misses (a run-in quote over about 100 words, or a block under about 40 words).
- A block quote: no quotation marks, indented as a whole (0.5 in is the guides' figure), with space above and below [S].
- Note number in a running sentence: superscript, after all punctuation except the dash (before a dash), at the end of the clause or sentence it supports [S].
- Note number after a block quote: after the quote's final punctuation, outside any quotation marks [S]. A summary claiming "the logic flips" for block quotes was vague, and the final-punctuation rule is the supported one.
- Sources: https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/Quotations/faq0061.html (result, summary only) ; https://guides.lib.unc.edu/citing-information/chicago-notes-in-text ; https://www.uvu.edu/writingcenter/handouts/notes-and-bibliography.html

## 12. 17th vs 18th differences to flag (NB system)

| Item | 17th | 18th |
|---|---|---|
| Place of publication | `New York: Penguin, 2015` | Not required; `Penguin, 2015` [S] |
| Authors in a note | about 3, then first + et al. [U] | up to 2; more than 2 is first + et al. [S] |
| Authors in a bibliography | up to 10 [U] | up to 6; more than 6 is first 3 + et al. [S] |
| Repeated author in bib | 3-em dash | repeat the name [S] |
| Ibid. | discouraged, allowed [S] | discouraged, allowed; three short forms named [S] |
| Chapter page range in bib | required | optional [S] |
| URL and date | access date only if no date [U] | same, plus shortened-URL guidance [S] |
| AI and Indigenous sources | none | new guidance [S] (out of scope for humanities essays) |

## 13. Ten most common Chicago-notes errors (reviewer scan list)

1. **Wrong system or mixed systems:** parenthetical (Author Year) or MLA `(Author 25)` inside a notes essay, or no notes at all for a paraphrase.
2. **Note number placement:** before the period, mid-sentence far from the claim, inside the block quote, or after a dash.
3. **Full note repeated** on later citations, or a short note used on the first citation of a source.
4. **Ibid. misuse:** non-adjacent, preceded by a multi-source note, lowercase, or ibid. used to cite a different page without a page number.
5. **Note vs bibliography format confused:** inverted names in notes, commas/parentheses in the bibliography (notes use commas and `(Publisher, Year)`; bib uses periods and inverted first author).
6. **Bibliography faults:** not alphabetised by last name, no hanging indent, cited sources missing, or uncited sources listed.
7. **Page numbers:** none on a quotation or specific claim, `p.`/`pp.` prefix, hyphen where en dash belongs, badly elided ranges, or whole-range pages in a note.
8. **Translations:** translator omitted, original-language title used with English pages, or the original's pagination cited when the translation was read.
9. **Titles:** books and journals not italic, articles and chapters not in quotation marks, no headline-style capitalisation, or a short title that does not match the full entry.
10. **Online and block-quote slips:** bare or incomplete web citations (no author or site, no date or access date, hyperlink text instead of URL, non-`https://doi.org/` DOI), and block quotes set in quotation marks, or a long quote run in.

Supporting list: https://guides.vpcc.edu/chicago/errors (summary matches items 3, 4, 5, 6).
