## 1.1 In-text citation (author-page, no year)

| Case | Form | Source |
|---|---|---|

## 1.2 Works Cited entries (alphabetical by first element, hanging indent 0.5 in, heading "Works Cited")

Template skeleton (core elements): Author. Title of source. Title of container, other contributors, version, number, publisher, date, location.

**Book, single author.** [Purdue books](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_works_cited_page_books.html), [MLA books](https://style.mla.org/works-cited/citations-by-format/books/)
- Template: Last, First. *Title: Subtitle*. Publisher, Year.
- Example (invented): Hale, Marguerite. *Gardens of the Northern Coast*. Harbor Press, 2012.
- Official shape: Gleick, James. *Chaos: Making a New Science*. Penguin, 1987. City of publication is dropped (only for pre-1900 books or unknown publishers).
- Editions go before publisher: 3rd ed., Pearson, 2004. Two publishers are joined by a slash.

**Translated book.** [Purdue books](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_works_cited_page_books.html), [original publication info](https://style.mla.org/original-publication-information/), [reprints](https://style.mla.org/reprinted-books/)
- Template (work emphasised): Last, First. *Title*. Translated by First Last, Publisher, Year. "Translated by" sits after the title.
- Example (invented): Veliko, Marta. *The Salt Road*. Translated by Jonas Reed, Meridian Books, 1998.
- Official shape: Foucault, Michel. *Madness and Civilization*. Translated by Richard Howard, Vintage-Random House, 1988.
- Original year is optional and goes in a supplemental slot, either right after the title (*Title*. 1959. Publisher, 2017.) or at the end
  (Publisher, 2017. Originally published as *Original Title* (Publisher, 2002).). The translation year stays the main date.
- Translator-focused form (translator first, "translator," after name, "By Author" after title) is implied by the same pages but
  not shown in the summaries: UNCONFIRMED wording.
- In-text uses the author only: (Veliko 77). The translator is not named in the parenthetical.

**Chapter or essay in an edited volume.** [Purdue books](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_works_cited_page_books.html)
- Template: Last, First. "Chapter Title." *Book Title*, edited by First Last, Publisher, Year, pp. xx-yy.
- Example (invented): Ortiz, Daniel. "Lighthouses and the Language of Warning." *Coastal Histories*, edited by Ruth Pell, Harbor Press, 2015, pp. 55-72.
- Official: Harris, Muriel. "Talk to Me: Engaging Reluctant Writers." *A Tutor's Guide: Helping Writers One to One*, edited by Ben Rafoth, Heinemann, 2000, pp. 24-34.
- In-text cites the chapter author, not the editor: (Ortiz 60).

**Journal article, print and DOI/URL.** [Purdue periodicals](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_works_cited_periodicals.html), [page range and DOI](https://style.mla.org/page-range-and-doi/)
- Template: Last, First. "Article Title." *Journal*, vol. 12, no. 3, Year, pp. xx-yy. Add `https://doi.org/...` last when online.
- Print (invented): Hale, Marguerite. "Tide Tables in Practice." *Journal of Coastal Studies*, vol. 12, no. 3, 2009, pp. 201-218.
- DOI (official shape): Wu, Yue, et al. "Bad Greenwashing, Good Greenwashing." *Management Science*, vol. 66, no. 7, 2020, pp. 3095-3112, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3340.
- Location priority online: DOI, then permalink, then URL. DOI as full https://doi.org/ link; ordinary URLs are given without "https://".
  Labels vol., no., pp. are required (they are not optional as in APA). Three or more authors: first author plus et al. in the list.

**Web page or online post.** [MLA online works](https://style.mla.org/works-cited/citations-by-format/online-works/), [Purdue electronic](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_works_cited_electronic_sources.html)
- Template: Author. "Page Title." *Website*, Publisher (omit if same as site), Day Mon. Year, URL. Accessed Day Mon. Year.
- Example (invented): Pell, Ruth. "How to Read a Tide Chart." *Harbor Notes*, 4 Mar. 2021, harbornotes.example.org/tide-charts. Accessed 12 June 2025.
- Official shape: Lundman, Susan. "How to Make Vegetarian Chili." *eHow*, www.ehow.com/how_10727_make-vegetarian-chili.html. Accessed 6 July 2025.
- Access date is optional but advised for undated or changing pages. Month abbreviation rule (Jan., Feb., Mar., Sept.; May, June, July in full)
  is background knowledge, not re-verified this session.

**Repeated author.** Handbook 9 allows three hyphens (---) for the same author in consecutive entries, ordered by title; MLA says future
editions will repeat the name in full and accepts either now. [MLA accessibility note](https://style.mla.org/accessibility-em-dashes-in-entries/)

## 1.3 Classic works with standard divisions

- Confirmed general MLA rule: for works in many editions, give your edition's page, a semicolon, then the division (ch., bk., pt.);
  for verse and plays use period-separated divisions. [Purdue basics](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_in_text_citations_the_basics.html)
- Plato (Stephanus), Aristotle (Bekker), Kant (A/B): no official MLA page naming these was found. UNCONFIRMED as MLA policy.
  Philosophy-writing practice (not MLA-specific): (Plato, *Republic* 514a); (Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 1094a1-5); (Kant, *Critique* A51/B75).
  Standard-number background: [Proofed on Stephanus/Bekker](https://proofed.com/writing-tips/citing-plato-and-aristotle-stephanus-and-bekker-numbers/).
- Reviewer policy to propose: do NOT flag standard numbering in place of page numbers when the Works Cited entry names the translation used.
  DO flag a missing Works Cited entry (MLA always lists the edition or translation consulted). Do not flag the "a/b" letters as a page-format error.
- MLA states that a quoted translation you made yourself takes "my trans." in the parenthetical. [MLA Style Center](https://style.mla.org/when-to-use-my-trans/)

## 1.4 Block quotations

[Purdue quotations](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_formatting_quotations.html)
- Threshold: more than four lines of prose, or more than three lines of verse. Shorter quotes run in with double quotation marks; verse
  line breaks in run-in quotes are marked with / (and // for a stanza break).
- Format: starts on a new line, whole block indented 0.5 in, no quotation marks, double-spaced, verse keeps original line breaks.
- Citation goes AFTER the final punctuation: ... end of quote. (Hale 41) with no period after the parenthesis.

## 1.5 Eight common MLA errors a reviewer can scan for

1. Comma or "p." in the parenthetical: (Hale, p. 41) should be (Hale 41). [source summaries: Purdue and MLA Style Center pages]
2. A year in the parenthetical, (Hale 2012, 41): Chicago or APA bleed-through. MLA cites author and page only.
3. Three or more authors not shortened to first author plus et al., in text or in the list; "&" used instead of "and".
4. Same author cited more than once with no short title to tell the works apart.
5. Invented page or paragraph numbers for unpaginated sources, or a redundant parenthetical when the author is already named and no number exists.
6. Indirect source cited as if read directly (missing "qtd. in"), or the original author listed in Works Cited instead of the source consulted.
7. Works Cited faults: not alphabetical, no hanging indent, entries never cited (or in-text names with no entry), year placed after the author.
8. Element and format slips: missing vol./no./pp. labels, "DOI:" label or "https://" kept on non-DOI URLs, missing "Translated by" or "edited by",
   book titles in quotes or article titles in italics, block quote with quotation marks or the citation before the final period.

## 2.2 Reference list entries (heading "References", alphabetical, year right after the author, hanging indent)

Edition differences confirmed by several university summaries ([UQ](https://guides.library.uq.edu.au/referencing/chicago-18th-author-date/reference-lists)):
CMOS 18 drops place of publication for books, no longer requires a chapter page range, repeats author names instead of a 3-em dash,
and lists up to six authors (more than six: first three plus et al.). CMOS 17 keeps "City: Publisher" and the 3-em dash.
The CMOS 17 author thresholds (list up to ten, else first seven plus et al.) are background knowledge only: UNCONFIRMED this session.
Same-author entries are in chronological order.

**Book, single author.** [UQ book](https://guides.library.uq.edu.au/referencing/chicago-18th-author-date/book)
- 18th: Hale, Marguerite. 2012. *Gardens of the Northern Coast*. Harbor Press. (invented)
- 17th: Hale, Marguerite. 2012. *Gardens of the Northern Coast*. Boston: Harbor Press. (invented)
- In-text: (Hale 2012, 41).

**Translated book.** [Monash books](https://guides.lib.monash.edu/chicago-18-b-author-date/books-and-chapters)
- Template: Last, First. Year of the edition used. *Title*. Translated by First Last. Publisher.
- Example (invented): Veliko, Marta. 1998. *The Salt Road*. Translated by Jonas Reed. Meridian Books.
- Official shape: García Márquez, Gabriel. 1988. *Love in the Time of Cholera*. Translated by Edith Grossman. Cape. In-text (García Márquez 1988, 242-255).
- Translator appears only in the list; the in-text citation names the author. The date is the translation's year. Showing the original year
  uses the reprint pattern ((1893) 1967); whether CMOS prescribes this for translations specifically is UNCONFIRMED.

**Chapter in an edited volume.** [Monash](https://guides.lib.monash.edu/chicago-18-b-author-date/books-and-chapters)
- Template: Last, First. Year. "Chapter Title." In *Book Title*, edited by First Last, pp-range. Publisher.
- 18th (invented): Ortiz, Daniel. 2015. "Lighthouses and the Language of Warning." In *Coastal Histories*, edited by Ruth Pell, 55-72. Harbor Press.
- 17th adds "City:" and expects the page range. "edited by" is spelled out (not "ed."). In-text cites the chapter author: (Ortiz 2015, 60).

**Journal article, print and DOI/URL.** [UQ journal](https://guides.library.uq.edu.au/referencing/chicago-18th-author-date/journal-article)
- Template: Last, First. Year. "Article Title." *Journal* volume (issue): pages. https://doi.org/...
- Print (invented): Hale, Marguerite. 2009. "Tide Tables in Practice." *Journal of Coastal Studies* 12 (3): 201-218.
- DOI (official): Burns, Timothy. 2015. "Philosophy and Poetry: A New Look at an Old Quarrel." *The American Political Science Review* 109 (2): 326-338. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055415000076.
- DOI is written as a https://doi.org/ link, never "DOI:". The list gives the whole page range; the in-text cite gives the specific page.

**Web page or online post.** [Curtin websites](https://uniskills.library.curtin.edu.au/referencing/chicago18/websites-and-social-media)
- Template: Author or Organisation. Year. "Page Title." Website or Owner. URL.
- Dated (invented): Pell, Ruth. 2021. "How to Read a Tide Chart." Harbor Notes. https://harbornotes.example.org/tide-charts.
- Undated (official shape): DevelopmentWA. n.d. "Yagan Square." DevelopmentWA. Accessed April 8, 2020. http://www.developmentwa.com.au/projects/redevelopment/yagan-square/overview.
- Accessed date is added for undated pages. Date style is spelled-out month, day, year.

## 2.4 Block quotations

[CMOS Q&A](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/Quotations/faq0061.html), [Purdue 18 general format](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/cmos_formatting_and_style_guide/general_format.html)
- Threshold: about 100 words or more, or five or more lines (soft guidance: "can" be blocked, so do not treat a 90-word run-in quote as an error).
  The Q&A page cites old numbering (12.10); current-edition wording UNCONFIRMED.
- Format: new line, indented with the indent tool, no quotation marks, quote stays in the same paragraph style.
- Author-date citation goes after the final punctuation: ... end of quote. (Hale 2012, 41) with no period after it.
- Poetry: two or more lines may be blocked (background, UNCONFIRMED).

## 2.5 Eight common Chicago author-date errors a reviewer can scan for

1. Comma between author and year: (Hale, 2012) should be (Hale 2012); or no comma before page: (Hale 2012 41) should be (Hale 2012, 41).
2. "p." or "pp." in the parenthetical: (Hale 2012, p. 41).
3. Footnotes or superscripts used for citation (notes-bibliography habit), or a "Bibliography" heading instead of "References".
4. Year at the end of the list entry (notes-bibliography placement) instead of directly after the author.
5. In-text citation with no matching list entry (or the reverse), year mismatch between text and list, or missing a/b suffix for same author and year.
6. Wrong et al. threshold: four or more authors must be shortened in text; the list shows all names (up to the edition's limit, 6 in CMOS 18).
7. Edition mixing in one paper: "City: Publisher" beside no-place entries, or 3-em dashes beside repeated names. Flag inconsistency, not the choice.
8. Format slips: "ed." or "trans." in list entries (CMOS spells out "edited by", "Translated by"), "DOI:" label or a bare doi.org link without https://,
   undated web page without "n.d." and "Accessed", chapter author replaced by the editor in text.

# PART 3. STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCES: KEEP THE STYLES APART

Chicago notes-bibliography (NB) cites in footnotes or endnotes, with a "Bibliography" and the date near the end of each entry. See
[Skidmore summary](https://libanswers.skidmore.edu/faq/196363) and [Purdue 18 NB guide](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/chicago_manual_18th_edition/documents/chicagonb-202508071.pdf).

| Feature | MLA 9 | Chicago author-date | Chicago notes-bibliography |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-text device | Parenthetical (Hale 41) | Parenthetical (Hale 2012, 41) | Superscript number, note at foot or end |
| End list heading | Works Cited | References | Bibliography |
| Date in list | Near end, with container info | Directly after author | Near end, after publisher |
| Page label in list | vol., no., pp. required | No labels: 12 (3): 201-218 | No labels (volume, issue in parentheses, page after colon) |
| Translator or editor | "Translated by", "edited by" | same, spelled out | "Translated by" in bibliography, "trans." in notes |
| Same-author entries | --- or repeat name (either, now) | Chronological, repeat name (CMOS 18) | Alphabetical by title, repeat name in CMOS 18 |
| Web access date | Optional | Only if undated | Only if undated or required |

Fast detection rules for a reviewer:
- Superscript numbers or note text means NB; flag as a mixing error if the assignment asked for author-date or MLA.
- "(Name Year" means Chicago author-date (also APA-like; Chicago uses a comma before page, no "p.").
- "(Name 41)" or "(Name qtd. in Name 41)" means MLA; "vol.", "no.", "pp." in entries is also MLA-only.
- A list with the year right after the author is author-date; the year near the end is MLA or NB.
- Both Chicago systems and MLA accept a spelled-out "Translated by" and a block quote of the same construction; only the citation shape differs.
- Decide style from the assignment prompt first, then flag only deviations from that style.
