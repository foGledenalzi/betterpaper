# filler-phrases: phrase-level padding candidates for the filler search (run by the voice-echo-reviewer; hits are charged to Mechanics by the orchestrator)
# Grammar: one lowercase phrase per line. A line that starts with # is a comment and is never searched. The file has no blank lines.
# A trailing ? marks a conditional entry. Drop the ? before searching. A hit is padding only when no source follows: no note marker, parenthetical citation or named author or opponent in that sentence.
# Every entry without a ? is unconditional. Search case-insensitively, skip text inside quotation marks and block quotations, and search the curly form of an apostrophe as well as the straight one.
# What the list holds: multi-word padding phrases from general writing guidance, judged as padding whoever wrote the text.
# What it excludes: single words, and any word that is only thought to sound machine-written (legitimate words, and flagging them hits careful and non-native writers hardest).
# It also excludes legitimate signposts that tell the reader where the argument goes: however, by contrast, on the other hand, first, second, in sum, in conclusion, this essay argues.
# Padding at paragraph level (a paragraph that repeats earlier material or does no work) is outside this list: it belongs to Structure and is judged by reading.
# A match is only a candidate. Read the sentence and confirm that cutting the phrase, or replacing it with one word, loses nothing. Report a confirmed hit as filler with its locator; never grade by the number of hits.
# Never say or imply who or what wrote the text.
