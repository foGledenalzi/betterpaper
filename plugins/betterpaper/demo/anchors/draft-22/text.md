# Honest Weights at Harnsey Staithe: The Pier-Scale and the Fish Market

The pier-scale at Harnsey Staithe is an iron beam balance bolted to the end of the landing stage, and for forty years it has weighed every catch sold on the quay. This essay argues that the scale kept the market honest chiefly because every buyer could see it, and only secondarily because the harbour inspector stamped its weights. The claim matters because most accounts of fair dealing at small ports credit the inspector first.

The market ledger gives the clearest evidence.[^1] Before 2016 the scale stood inside the fish shed, where only the weigher and the seller could see the beam. In that year the harbour board moved it to the open end of the stage. In the twelve months before the move the ledger records 41 disputes over weight; in the twelve months after, it records 9. The same weigher, the same weights and the same inspector were in post throughout.

Meanwhile the inspector began his weekly visits in the spring of 2017. It might be objected that the fall in disputes came from the inspector's visits and not from the move, since both belong to the same period of reform. The ledger answers part of this. Disputes had already fallen to 12 a month before the first visit, so the visits cannot explain the whole of the change. They may still have helped, because buyers knew that visits were planned, and the ledger cannot show whether that expectation, rather than the open stage, did the work. The essay can say only that visibility was sufficient for most of the fall.

The pier-scale therefore teaches a modest lesson. A weighing is trusted when those who pay for it can watch it done, and an inspector adds assurance at the margin. Where a small port must choose where to spend, the open stage comes first.

[^1]: Harnsey Staithe Fish Market, *Ledger of Weighings and Disputes, 2015-17*, Harbour Board archive.
