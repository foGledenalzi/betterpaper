## MAJOR PROBLEMS, IN ORDER OF SEVERITY

### 1. THE ESSAY STATES THE RELATION BETWEEN MARK AND READING IN THE OPPOSITE ORDER FROM THE TREATISE [PRIM-001]
- Locator: para 2, Karvale's account begins. Trigger: "a reading fixes what the mark asks"
- What it does, with its steelman (the strongest reading the essay's own material supports, not a rewrite): the passage says how a reading relates to a mark. Read generously, it means that the level of the grit shows which question was put, as the essay's own thesis speaks of "an answer fixed by the question its scale asks".
- Basis: the treatise says "Each mark asks a question of the stack; a reading only answers it", so the mark sets the question and the reading does not (checked against the original (first edition, 11)).
- Consequence: the thesis depends on the direction from question to answer, and this passage runs the other way; the essay's account of the treatise and its thesis disagree on the one relation the thesis depends on.
- Why it ranks here: it is a tier 1 defect in the passage the thesis rests on, and it sets Command of the treatise at D (prim-d-1).

### 2. THE ESSAY USES TALLY WITHOUT ITS CONTRAST WITH COUNT [PRIM-002]
- Locator: para 2, Karvale's account begins. Trigger: "a tally, that is, a count of what the stack does"
- What it does: it defines the essay's central term. Paragraph 1 gives the same term as "an answer fixed by the question its scale asks", and the definition here gives it as a count of what the stack does. Both cannot stand: which is it?
- Basis: the treatise sets the two apart, "A tally is not a count" (checked against the original (first edition, 14)).
- Consequence: every later use of tally inherits the collapse, and the thesis that a reading is a tally cannot be told from the view it argues against (in my reading).

### 3. THE ESSAY USES SETTLE OF THE OBSERVER, WHERE THE TREATISE USES IT OF THE GRIT [PRIM-003]
- Locator: para 4, Consider a glass. Trigger: "the observer settles the reading"
- What it does: it gives the observer the act that fixes a reading. Read generously, it means only that the observer picks the mark, as the sentence before says: "The finger decides which mark the grit has reached".
- Basis: the treatise uses settle only of the grit coming to rest (checked against the original (first edition, 19)).
- Consequence: a reader who knows the treatise will take the sentence as a use of the term against its source, and paragraph 4 leans on it for the claim about uncalibrated glasses.

### 4. THE ESSAY ASSERTS WITHOUT SUPPORT THAT AN UNCALIBRATED GLASS GIVES A TALLY [ARG-001]
- Locator: para 4, Consider a glass. Trigger: "the same holds of a glass no one has ever calibrated"
- What it does: it carries the point about reading by touch over to glasses never checked against a known stack, and the last sentence builds on it: "the tally is there before any stack is met". Read generously, if "the touch is trained to the marks, not to the stack", checking against a stack could add nothing; the essay does not draw that step.
- Basis: no example, source or mechanism in the paragraph supports the clause (in my reading).
- Consequence: the clause carries the essay's reply to the objection from calibration, so without support the thesis claim of surviving that objection is not yet earned.
- Positioning flag: readers of a graduate seminar on measurement and meaning will likely ask what shows that an uncalibrated glass gives any tally at all.
- Why it ranks here: it sets Argument at C (arg-c-2).

### 5. THE ESSAY INFERS THAT NO READING CAN BE WRONG FROM THE AGREEMENT BETWEEN GLASSES, WITH NO STEP BETWEEN THEM [ARG-002]
- Locator: para 5, Agreement between glasses. Trigger: "It follows that no reading can be wrong."
- What it does: it draws a conclusion from the sentences before it, which say that "Two glasses cut from one pattern read alike because they ask alike". Read generously, the last sentence ("Each reading is the right answer to the question its own scale put") gives a sense in which no reading is wrong, as an answer to that question; the essay does not say so, and does not say what wrong means.
- Basis: the paragraph says nothing about what would make a reading wrong before the conclusion names it (in my reading).
- Consequence: the reader has to supply the step, and the account of a reading as an answer does not yet show that an answer cannot miss its question.
- Why it ranks here: with problem 4 it sets Argument at C (arg-c-1).

## PROOFREADING

M1 means spelling and agreement; M2 means syntax, how clauses join.
- Agreement (M1), para 2: "each of the marks ask": ask -> asks. A singular subject such as each takes a singular verb. [MECH-001]
- Agreement (M1), para 2: "the scale and the grit remains": remains -> remain. Two nouns joined by and take a plural verb. [MECH-001]
- Apostrophe (M1), para 3: "the glasses scale": glasses -> glass's. The scale belongs to one glass, so the possessive needs an apostrophe. [MECH-002]
- Comma splice (M2), para 6: "The glass settles, the reader waits." A comma splice means two complete clauses joined by only a comma, not simply a long sentence. Separate or join them with a mark that fits; the choice is yours. [MECH-003]
